OSCD Sentinel-2 Satellite Change Detection

Satellite change detection built from first principles on Sentinel-2 bitemporal imagery, pairing classical baselines with a compact Siamese U-Net. Every threshold is chosen on validation data alone, and every claim is labeled VERIFIED or UNVERIFIED — including four early claims that were later corrected on the record.
  • Dataset Provenance & Licensing: Sourced OSCD and LEVIR-CD directly from their original providers, with each dataset's licence tracked explicitly — LEVIR-CD is characterized but kept out of training to respect its stricter terms
  • Classical Detection Baselines, Built From Scratch: Absolute-difference, Change Vector Analysis, and PCA-K-means detectors plus spectral-index variants, establishing an honest classical floor of F1 0.237 before any learned model enters the comparison
  • Learned Architecture on Laptop-Scale Hardware: A ~269k-parameter Siamese U-Net (shared encoder, absolute-difference fusion, weighted BCE + soft Dice loss) reaches mean F1 0.378 across 4 seeds on a single machine with no GPU cluster — about 1.6x the classical floor
  • Threshold-Honest Evaluation Protocol: Thresholds are chosen on the validation split only, whole-image metrics are computed after stitching tiles back together, and results are reported per seed as a range (1.43x–1.71x) rather than one number
  • Radiometric Nuisance Characterization: Found that brightness drift between image dates (roughly −654 to +609 DN) is as large as the real change signal — a label-free correction for it lifted classical mean AUC from 0.660 to 0.830 across six detectors
  • Statistical Power Analysis: Measured spatial autocorrelation of 24x–270x across tiles, showing the effective sample size is closer to 1,192 than the raw tile count — a check most computer-vision projects skip
  • Label-Free Quantification Program: Named the open problem correctly — calibrated, label-free change-rate prediction — and tested strategies from the quantification literature, including Median Sweep, against it
  • Adversarial Self-Verification: Four of the project's own claims were later overturned and corrected on the record, backed by 135 tests including 5 deliberately-failing tripwires that catch regressions
